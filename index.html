Political economy · conflict · development

Jorge E. Zavala

I study how violence endures across generations—and when public policy can credibly interrupt those cycles.

I am a doctoral researcher in Political Science at Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin and a research fellow at the WZB Berlin Social Science Center. My work combines field experiments, quasi-experimental designs, and administrative and survey data to study conflict legacies, transitional justice, state–society relations, and development in Latin America and beyond.

Research agenda

Breaking cycles of violence

My dissertation asks how political violence travels from institutions and communities into households, beliefs, and life trajectories—and how material and informational forms of transitional justice can change those pathways.

The agenda connects political economy, development economics, and comparative politics. It is organized around post-conflict Peru and speaks to wider questions about recognition, legitimacy, migration, and the reach of the state.

01

Conflict legacies

How historical violence shapes present-day victimization, coercive norms, and intergenerational socialization.

02

Transitional justice

When reparations and public information generate recognition, institutional legitimacy, and durable repair.

03

Development & the state

How policy changes access, mobility, livelihoods, and citizen–state relations in places marked by conflict.

Dissertation chapter Quasi-experimental

Are Collective Reparations Transitional or Transformational?

Migration and Policy Feedback in Post-Conflict Peru

With Matthew Bird and Ana María Dumez. A fuzzy regression discontinuity design evaluates whether collective reparations repair state–society relations while transforming community trajectories through migration and expanded state access.
