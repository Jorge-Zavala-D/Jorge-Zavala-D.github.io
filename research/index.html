Research

Research on conflict legacies, transitional justice, political economy, and development.

Political economy · conflict · development

My research asks how violence and weak institutions shape behavior over time, and whether carefully designed public policy can generate recognition, legitimacy, and more inclusive development.

Dissertation

Breaking Cycles of Violence

Intergenerational Legacies of Conflict and Violence, and the Determinants of Effective Policy Pathways for Sustainable Peace and Development

The dissertation treats households, communities, and the state as connected sites through which violence is remembered, normalized, contested, or repaired. It brings together one observational paper on intergenerational transmission, one quasi-experimental paper on collective reparations, and one randomized field experiment on transitional-justice information.

Supervisors: Macartan Humphreys, Anselm Hager, and Alexandra Scacco.

Historical Conflict Exposure and the Intergenerational Transmission of Violence

Evidence from ENARES 2024 and Peru's Internal Armed Conflict

Did Peru's internal armed conflict leave traces in present-day victimization, tolerance of coercion, and violent socialization? The paper links historical conflict-intensity measures with nationally relevant survey data on violence against women, children, and adolescents. Its contribution is to connect long-run conflict research with the household and school environments through which violence may be reproduced—or rejected—across generations.

Are Collective Reparations Transitional or Transformational?

Migration and Policy Feedback in Post-Conflict Peru

With Matthew Bird and Ana María Dumez

Peru's Collective Reparations Program was designed to recognize collective harm and support local recovery. Because reparations often arrive as infrastructure or livelihood projects, however, they can also expand state presence, market access, and information flows. Using the victimization-index cutoff that prioritized communities, the paper estimates whether reparations strengthened state–society relations while also reshaping community trajectories through out-migration.

Design
Fuzzy regression discontinuity
Setting
Post-conflict Peru
Status
Working paper; forthcoming 2026
III
RecognitionCluster RCT

Recognition-through-Information?

Transitional Justice Knowledge, Legitimacy, and Rule-of-Law Orientations in Post-Conflict Peru

Can politically neutral information about transitional justice increase knowledge, perceived recognition, institutional legitimacy, rule-of-law orientations, and civic efficacy? A cluster-randomized saturation pilot in rural Ayacucho and San Martín evaluates direct and spillover effects while examining credibility, baseline knowledge, local victimization histories, and social diffusion.

Design
Cluster-randomized saturation pilot
Setting
Ayacucho and San Martín, Peru
Status
Pilot implemented; manuscript in progress

Mental health & peacebuildingNigeria

Evaluating CBT Group Interventions in Conflict-Affected Nigerian Communities

With Nora Chirikure, Macartan Humphreys, Madalena Kresimon, and Alexandra Scacco. A two-wave randomized evaluation across 24 communities in Kaduna, Plateau, and Taraba studies whether group-based cognitive behavioral therapy improves psychological recovery, daily functioning, social connection, and verified community participation.

1,152 priority participants · matched-block randomization · WZB / GIZ / IOM

Digital public infrastructureColombia

Digital Bridges for Financial Inclusion

With Angélica Cardozo. A mixed-methods study of how Venezuelan migrant women navigate identity, payments, remittances, fraud, and recourse in Colombia's evolving digital public infrastructure. Survey evidence, latent class analysis, interviews, focus groups, and a regional benchmark identify four distinct user profiles and the institutional constraints that keep digital inclusion partial.

Mixed methods · latent class analysis · supported by the Center for Financial Inclusion
